## What is competitive programming?

Competitive programming is solving well-defined puzzles with code, against the clock. Each problem gives you a short story, an input format and an output format. You write a program, submit it to an online **judge**, and within seconds it tells you whether your program is correct and fast enough on hidden test cases.

A typical problem might ask: *"Given the map of a city, what's the shortest route between two intersections?"* or *"How many ways can you make change for $5 using these coins?"* The trick is finding an idea, an **algorithm**, that solves it efficiently, then writing it without bugs.

If you see these verdicts, here's what they mean:

- **Accepted**: your program is correct and fast enough. 🎉
- **Wrong Answer**: it printed the wrong output on some test.
- **Time Limit Exceeded**: it's correct but too slow, so you need a better algorithm.
- **Run Time Error**: it crashed (out-of-bounds index, division by zero…).

You don't need to know any of this yet. That's what the club is for.

## Why it's worth your time

- **Interview prep.** Technical interviews for co-op, internships and new-grad jobs are mostly algorithm problems. Competitive programmers find them familiar.
- **Problem solving.** You learn to break a hard problem into pieces, reason about edge cases, and prove to yourself that an idea works before you code it.
- **Better code, faster.** You'll write clean, correct code quickly, and get much better at debugging.
- **ICPC.** The International Collegiate Programming Contest is the oldest and largest university programming contest in the world. Teams of three solve problems on one computer, and the best teams from each regional advance toward the World Finals.
- **Community.** It's more fun with friends. Practice nights, contests and post-contest debriefs are where you meet people who like the same things you do.

## ICPC and the East Central NA Regional

UWindsor competes in the **ICPC East Central North America (ECNA) Regional**, which brings together university teams from southern Ontario and the neighbouring U.S. states. UWindsor hosts the regional, so our students can compete at a major ICPC contest without leaving campus.

## Our mission

CPC UWindsor was started by three UWindsor ICPC contestants who want more students to discover competitive programming. Our goals are simple:

1. **Get more students started**, especially people who've never tried it, through beginner-friendly workshops and practice nights.
2. **Build a community** of people who enjoy solving problems together.
3. **Send more UWindsor teams to the ICPC ECNA Regional**, and help them do well there.

Everyone is welcome, whatever your program, year or experience.
